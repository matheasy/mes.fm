INTERNATIONALLY KNOWN INVENTOR JOHN HUTCHISON FIGHTS FOR HIS LIFE FOLLOWING CATASTROPHIC MEDICAL NEGLIGENCE ACROSS NORTHERN CALIFORNIA HEALTHCARE SYSTEMS

WILLITS, Calif., Nov. 12, 2026 — Formal regulatory complaints have been filed and logged with the California Department of Public Health (CDPH) and the Medical Board of California against four major Northern California healthcare networks—Adventist Health, Sutter Health, Kaiser Permanente, and John Muir Health—following catastrophic medical negligence that has left internationally recognized inventor John Hutchison fighting advanced bladder cancer and acute kidney decline.

Hutchison, whose electromagnetic and scientific experiments have appeared on more than 300 television broadcasts worldwide, survived a cascading chain of life-threatening medical failures—including withheld infection results, contraindicated cancer immunotherapy during an active Proteus infection, a 15-pound post-surgical bowel impaction, and an acute opioid overdose. The family has submitted the full chronological evidence packet into state regulatory databases and is seeking legal accountability for medical malpractice, gross negligence, patient civil rights violations, and fraudulent billing practices.

Coercion and Primary Care Neglect in Willits

The trouble began at the Adventist Health Redwood Clinic in Willits when Hutchison became a patient of family doctor, Dr. Claudia Petruncio. Because Hutchison is autistic and lives with severe anxiety, he must see a doctor every month to get his vital, long-term anxiety medication refills. During these visits, the stress of the clinic makes Hutchison's blood pressure spike—a well-known reaction called "white coat syndrome."

Instead of accepting the evidence of white coat syndrome, Petruncio threatened that if Hutchison did not start taking a heavy regimen of multiple different blood pressure pills, she would drop him as a patient, leaving him cut off from his monthly anxiety medication. The family was terrified and reported this coercive bullying to the clinic's office manager in writing, but their cries for help were completely ignored.

Hutchison already suffered from lifelong, severe constipation because his anxiety medications naturally slow down his digestion. The intense emotional terror triggered by the doctor's threats made his physical stress spike, causing his digestion to lock up completely. This massive, untreated backup altered his body's internal hormones, heavily inflaming his prostate until it swelled shut, completely blocking his ability to pass urine. Every visit to Petruncio was met with an argument about Hutchison’s blood pressure, with no referrals to specialists for his gastrointestinal and urinary issues.

Because Petruncio focused solely on blood pressure while ignoring his anxiety and bowel function, the situation escalated and Hutchison had to rely on emergency rooms for basic survival. This primary care failure forced Hutchison into living with a Foley catheter, which triggers recurring urinary tract infections (UTIs)—setting off a disastrous chain of hospital errors.

Sutter staff stated the catheter needed to be removed in one week and directed Hutchison to the urologist who placed the catheter in the hospital. Hutchison called for an appointment for catheter removal in one week, only to be told none were available. When he finally secured an appointment, staff told him upon arrival that it was only for a "meeting," not catheter removal. Over a month after placement, the Sutter urologist finally removed the catheter. A cystoscopy was performed, and Hutchison was sent home, without a catheter. No bladder scan or urine sample was taken. Upon arrival home, Hutchison ended up back in the Willits ER with a full-blown UTI caused by the prolonged delay in removing the catheter.

Although that infection resolved and he remained catheter-free for a few months, urinary retention returned at the end of 2025. Refusing to return to Sutter Health due to the prior abuse, and receiving no specialist referral from Petruncio, Hutchison was forced back into the Willits ER for help with urine retention. In early 2026, Hutchison visited the Willits ER five times in eight days because placed catheters either failed to drain or fell out.

However, staff held him in pre-op for two hours without administering his anti-anxiety medication.
