How the final grade calculator works

Your course grade is a weighted average: each part of the course counts for a share of the total. Once you know how much of the course is already graded, the grade you need on what is left follows from one formula:

needed on the final = (target − current × (100 − final weight) / 100) ÷ final weight × 100

For example, with a current grade of 78%, a final worth 40% and a target of 80%: 78 × 60 / 100 = 46.8 points are already banked, so you need (80 − 46.8) ÷ 40 × 100 = 83% on the final. If the answer is above 100% the target is out of reach; if it is zero or below, you have already secured it.

Questions

What grade do I need on my final to get an A?

Set the target to 90 (an A- on the common US scale) or 93 (an A), enter your current grade and the final’s weight, and read the result. The table lists every target from 40% to 100% at once.

Can I use it if my final is worth 100%?

Yes. With a weight of 100 your current grade does not matter: you need the target grade itself on the exam.

What if my course uses points instead of percentages?

Enter each assignment’s points earned and points possible as the score and out-of, and its share of the course as the weight. If every assignment simply counts by its points, use Even weights… or the Weighted Average Calculator.

How it is worked out

About the weighted average calculator

Although a weighted average calculator is often used to find an overall semester average for courses that count for different numbers of credits, the same calculation works for any group of numbers: assignments, tests, courses or whole semesters. It can also give a plain, unweighted average: set every weight to the same value (the Equal weights button sets them all to 1).

How does it work?

A basic average adds up the grades and divides by how many there are. A weighted average multiplies each grade by its weight, adds those up, and divides by the total weight:

  • Basic average = (Grade1 + Grade2 + …) ÷ (number of grades)
  • Weighted average = (Grade1 × Weight1 + Grade2 × Weight2 + …) ÷ (Weight1 + Weight2 + …)

For example, grades of 90, 80 and 70 with credits 3, 4 and 2 give (90×3 + 80×4 + 70×2) ÷ 9 = 730 ÷ 9 = 81.11, a little higher than the plain average of 80 because the 90 counts for more than the 70.

Good to know

  • Percentage weights (e.g. 20%, 30%, 50%) work the same way; they do not have to add up to 100.

Weighted average. Weighted average = Σ(grade × weight) ÷ Σweight. Only the weights relative to each other matter, so credits, hours, points or percentages all work.
