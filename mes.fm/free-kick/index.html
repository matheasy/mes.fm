    The physics of a free kick

    Every kick is simulated a thousand times a second. Three forces act on the ball in the air:

    • Gravity pulls it down at 9.81 m/s².
    • Air drag slows it down. A football's drag changes with speed: below about 12 m/s the air flows smoothly around it and the drag is high; at shooting speeds the boundary layer turns turbulent and the drag nearly halves (the "drag crisis"). That is why a hard shot keeps its pace and a soft one dies.
    • The Magnus force pushes a spinning ball sideways: side spin curls it, top spin makes it dip, back spin makes it float. The force grows with the spin and with the speed, so a curler bends most late in its flight, once it has slowed down relative to its spin.

    With almost no spin at high speed, the air peels off the ball unevenly and the ball wobbles from side to side: the knuckleball. Keepers hate it because they can't predict it.

    The ball bounces off the ground (it loses about half its speed on each bounce), the posts and the bar, the wall players (who can jump) and the keeper, who can reach about 3.25 m from where he stands and needs about 0.12 s plus 0.085 s per metre to get his hands there after deciding to move.

    Rules used

    • A penalty is taken from 11 m. A shootout is five kicks each, taking turns; if one side can no longer catch up it ends early. Still level after five each: sudden death, one kick each until one scores and the other doesn't.
    • At a free kick the wall must stand at least 9.15 m from the ball. The number of players is up to the defending side; the "Auto" wall uses what most teams do (5 from close and central, 2 from wide or far out).
    • The whole ball must cross the whole goal line between the posts and under the bar for a goal.

    Free kicks, penalties and set pieces with real ball physics

    Take free kicks from any spot outside the box, curl them around the wall or dip them over it, smash knuckleballs from 30 metres, and settle it with a penalty shootout. Play the computer at four levels, a friend on the same device, someone online with a link, or another Hive user with every kick signed on the blockchain. You can be the kicker or the goalkeeper: as the keeper, click where to dive while the ball is in the air, or guess before the kick like keepers do at penalties.

    How to take a free kick

    1. Choose Free-kick practice and drag the ball on the small pitch (or press Random spot).
    2. Click or tap in the goal where you want the ball to go. The dotted line shows its path (turn the guide off for a real test).
    3. Set the power, the curl (side spin) and the dip (top spin; negative = back spin, the ball floats). Curl does not change where you aim: it changes the path, so you can bend the ball around the wall and still hit your spot.
    4. Hold Shoot (or Space): the power bar swings up and down. Let go at the power you want (the white mark is the slider's target). Prefer the slider? Set Power to "Use the slider".

    Questions

    How fast is a free kick?
    Good free kicks leave the boot at 25 to 30 m/s (90 to 110 km/h). Penalties are usually 20 to 28 m/s. The fastest shots measured are over 40 m/s.
    Why does the ball curve?
    Spin. The ball drags air round with it, so the air leaves the ball deflected to one side and the ball is pushed the other way: the Magnus effect. The same effect makes a tennis ball with top spin dip.
    What is a knuckleball?
    A hard shot with almost no spin. The air separates from the seams and panels at slightly different places from moment to moment, so the ball moves unpredictably in the air. Try the Knuckleball style: power over 27 m/s, curl and dip at 0.
    Where should I aim a penalty?
    High in a corner is almost unstoppable but easy to miss; low and hard into the corner is the safe choice. Keepers dive early at penalties, so a soft shot down the middle (a "Panenka") works if the keeper guesses, and looks silly if he stays.
    How does the computer keeper work?
    It watches the ball: after its reaction time (0.33 s on Easy down to 0.18 s on Pro, a little longer when a wall blocks its view) it dives to where it thinks the ball will cross the line, with less error on the higher levels. At penalties it sometimes guesses a side before the kick, like real keepers.
    How do online and Hive matches stay fair?
    The keeper chooses a dive without seeing the shot. In guest matches mes.fm's server holds both choices until both are in and adds the aim spread from a secret number. On Hive the keeper posts a sealed dive first, so the kicker can't see it, and opens the seal after the kick.
