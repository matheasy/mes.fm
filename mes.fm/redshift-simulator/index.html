Redshift and blueshift explained

A redshift lengthens the wavelength of light, a blueshift shortens it. Astronomers measure it as z = (λobserved − λemitted) / λemitted; a positive z is red, a negative z is blue. There are three physical causes, and this page simulates all of them:

  • Motion (Doppler shift). A source moving away is redshifted, one moving toward you is blueshifted: 1 + z = √((1 + β) / (1 − β)) along the line of sight, and the general formula fobs = fsrc / (γ(1 − β cos θ)). Even sideways motion gives a redshift 1 / γ because the moving source's clock runs slow.
  • Gravity. Near a mass clocks run slow, by √(1 − rs/r) with rs = 2GM/c². Light climbing out of a gravity well is redshifted, light falling in is blueshifted: fobs/fsrc = √((1 − rs/rsrc) / (1 − rs/robs)). The 1959 Pound-Rebka experiment measured it across 22.5 metres; GPS satellites must correct for it every day.
  • Expansion of space. Light from distant galaxies is stretched as the universe grows: 1 + z = anow/athen. At z = 1089 the light is the cosmic microwave background.

The refractive-index picture

Because clocks tick slower near a mass, a wave whose frequency is fixed in far-away time has a shorter wavelength nearby, just as light entering glass keeps its frequency and shortens its wavelength. Treating gravity as a medium with index n = 1 / (clock rate) reproduces the redshift, the slowing of light near a mass (the Shapiro delay) and light bending. It is an analogy for the Schwarzschild geometry, not a separate theory, and the animation exaggerates the tiny real numbers so you can see them (switch to True scale for the real picture).

Questions

Is a cosmological redshift a Doppler shift?
Not exactly. Galaxies are nearly at rest in their own patch of space; the space between them grows. For small z the two agree (z ≈ v/c); for large z they differ, and the recession speed in the table can even pass the speed of light, which is allowed because nothing moves through space that fast.
Why does gravitational redshift matter for GPS?
Satellite clocks run about 45.7 µs a day fast from gravity and 7.2 µs slow from their speed, a net 38.5 µs a day. Uncorrected, positions would drift by about 10 km a day.
Why can nothing be seen leaving a black hole?
As the emitter approaches the horizon (r = rs) the redshift grows without limit, so the light is stretched to nothing and arrives later and later.

Limits: static emitters and receivers in the Schwarzschild geometry (no spin, no orbital Doppler mixed in), a flat ΛCDM universe without radiation (the age at very high z is approximate), the spectral lines are laboratory values to 0.1 nm. For research use real ephemerides and models.
