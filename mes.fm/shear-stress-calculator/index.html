Where the numbers come from

These are typical room-temperature values for learning and first estimates. They change with the exact alloy, heat treatment, temper, bar size, moisture (wood, nylon), direction (wood, composites, bone) and supplier. Yield and tensile strengths of the A36 steel are the specification minimums. Elongation is in a 50 mm (2 in) gauge length. Reduction of area is a rough figure that only shapes the neck in the tensile test.

Sources: published supplier data sheets (Special Metals for nickel alloys, mill and distributor sheets for steels, aluminium and copper alloys), the ASTM / UNS property collections summarised by MatWeb and MakeItFrom, ASM handbooks, the USDA Forest Products Laboratory Wood Handbook (clear wood at 12% moisture), ACI 318 and Eurocode 2 (EN 1992-1-1, table 3.1) figures for concrete, the Portland Cement Association and the Brick Industry Association for concrete, mortar and brick, rock-mechanics tables (Goodman, Introduction to Rock Mechanics) for stone, the Asphalt Institute for asphalt, ceramic makers' data sheets (CoorsTek, Kyocera) for technical ceramics, Gent's Engineering with Rubber and rubber compounders' data for elastomers (hardness-graded compounds: real compounds vary a lot), textbook tables (Hibbeler, Gere & Goodno, Callister) and biomechanics reviews for bone. Shear stress, shear strain and torsion calculator

Work out the shear stress in a bolt or pin in single or double shear, a glued or welded lap joint, or the force to punch a hole; the shear strain and shear modulus; and, on the Torsion tab, the shear stress and angle of twist of a solid or hollow shaft from a torque or from the power and speed of a motor. Each tab gives a safety factor against the material's shear strength and finds the pin or shaft size you need for the safety factor you want.

Direct shear

  • Shear stress is the force across a plane divided by its area: \(\tau = V / A\). For a round pin \(A = \pi d^2 / 4\).
  • Single shear: two plates overlap and the bolt could be cut at one plane. Double shear: a clevis holds the middle plate between two outer ones, the pin is cut at two planes and the stress is halved, \(\tau = V / (2A)\).
  • Bearing stress is the pin pressing on the side of its hole, \(\sigma_b = V / (d\,t)\). Thin plates often fail this way first.
  • Shear strain is the change of angle of a small square, \(\gamma = \tau / G\), with the shear modulus \(G = E / (2(1+\nu))\) for a material that is the same in every direction.
  • Shear strength: a ductile metal yields in shear at about \(0.577\,\sigma_y\) (the von Mises rule, 1/√3); the ultimate shear strength used for punching is about 0.75 times the tensile strength for steel (you can change the factor).

Torsion of shafts

A torque T twists a shaft; the shear stress grows straight out from the middle and is highest at the surface: \(\tau_{max} = T c / J\) with \(c = D/2\) and the polar moment of area \(J = \pi D^4 / 32\) (solid) or \(J = \pi (D^4 - d^4) / 32\) (hollow). The angle of twist is \(\theta = T L / (G J)\) in radians. A motor's torque comes from its power and speed: \(T = P / \omega\) with \(\omega = 2\pi n / 60\) for n in rpm.

Worked examples

M10 bolt in single shear, 10 kN. A = π × 10² ÷ 4 = 78.54 mm², so τ = 10,000 ÷ 78.54 = 127.3 MPa. (Use the bolt's shank diameter only if the threads are not in the shear plane.)

16 mm clevis pin in double shear, 30 kN. Two planes of 201.1 mm² each: τ = 30,000 ÷ (2 × 201.1) = 74.6 MPa. In A36 steel (shear yield ≈ 0.577 × 250 = 144 MPa) the safety factor is about 1.9.

Punching a 20 mm hole in 3 mm A36 plate. The sheared area is the wall of the hole, π × 20 × 3 = 188.5 mm². With a shear strength of 0.75 × 400 = 300 MPa the punch needs 56.5 kN, about 5.8 tonnes-force.

50 mm solid steel shaft, 1 kN·m. J = π × 50⁴ ÷ 32 = 613,592 mm⁴, so τmax = 1,000,000 N·mm × 25 mm ÷ 613,592 mm⁴ = 40.7 MPa. Over 1 m with G = 79.3 GPa it twists 0.0206 rad, 1.18°.

A 7.5 kW motor at 1450 rpm turns at ω = 151.8 rad/s, so it gives T = 7,500 ÷ 151.8 = 49.4 N·m.

Questions

Single or double shear: which is stronger?

Double shear. The same pin carries twice the force at the same stress, because the load is shared by two cross-sections. It also keeps the pin from bending, which is why clevis joints are used for heavy loads.

Is the shear stress the same across the whole pin?

No: V/A is the average. In a beam the shear stress peaks in the middle of the section (4/3 of the average for a round bar). Pin and bolt design uses the average with a safety factor, as here.

Why is a hollow shaft lighter for the same strength?

The middle of a solid shaft carries almost no stress. Taking it away removes a lot of weight but little of J. A tube with an inside diameter of 80% of the outside is 64% lighter but has only 41% less J (so about 69% more stress at the same torque and outside diameter); make it a little bigger and it is both lighter and stronger than the solid shaft.

What is the shear modulus of steel?

About 77 to 80 GPa (11,200 to 11,600 ksi). From E = 200 GPa and ν = 0.3 the formula gives 76.9 GPa.
