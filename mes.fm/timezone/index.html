Time Zone Converter

Pick a time and a city, and see what that same moment is everywhere else. Daylight saving time is applied automatically for every zone. It opens on the current time in Richmond / Vancouver, BC (Pacific Time) — tap Use my location to switch to wherever you are, or edit the date and time to plan ahead.

        Copied ✓

        Everything runs in your browser. “Use my location” reads only your device’s time-zone setting — no GPS prompt, nothing sent anywhere.

        How to use the time zone converter

        • Set the date and time, then choose the city that time is in (it starts on Pacific Time for Richmond / Vancouver, BC).
        • Every card below updates to show that exact moment in another city, with the weekday, date and a +1 day / −1 day badge when the date rolls over.
        • Use Add a city / time zone to track more places, and the × on a card to remove one. Your list is remembered on this device.
        • Copy shareable link makes a URL that reopens the converter with the same time and cities — handy for scheduling calls and meetings.

        About Pacific Time (Vancouver & Richmond, BC)

        Richmond, British Columbia keeps the same clock as Vancouver, Seattle and Los Angeles: North American Pacific Time. From roughly mid-March to early November it observes Pacific Daylight Time (PDT), which is UTC−7; the rest of the year it is Pacific Standard Time (PST), UTC−8. This converter switches between PDT and PST for you based on the date you enter, so a summer conversion and a winter conversion of “1:00 PM Vancouver” give different UTC results — as they should.

        Common conversions from 1:00 PM in Vancouver

        During PDT (summer), 1:00 PM in Vancouver is about 4:00 PM in New York and Toronto, 9:00 PM in London, 10:00 PM in Paris and Berlin, 1:30 AM the next day in Mumbai, and 5:00 AM the next day in Tokyo. In winter (PST) each of those shifts an hour later. The live cards above always show the exact result for the date you pick.

        Does this handle daylight saving time?

        Yes. All conversions are computed from the official IANA time-zone database that ships with your browser, so DST start/end dates, historical changes and permanent-DST regions are all accounted for automatically.
