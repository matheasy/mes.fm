Trigonometry in one page

Trigonometry links the angles of a triangle to the lengths of its sides. In a right triangle: sin θ = opposite ÷ hypotenuse, cos θ = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse, tan θ = opposite ÷ adjacent (remember SOH-CAH-TOA). The reciprocals are csc = 1/sin, sec = 1/cos and cot = 1/tan. On the unit circle (radius 1), the point at angle θ is (cos θ, sin θ), which extends the functions to every angle, positive or negative.

The laws for any triangle

Law of sines: a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C. Law of cosines: c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C. Use the Solve triangles tab for SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS and the ambiguous SSA case (two possible triangles).

Questions

Should my calculator be in degrees or radians?
Use degrees for everyday angles and geometry; radians (2π = 360°) in calculus and physics. Type 3pi/4 or “2 rad” here and the unit is detected for you.
Why is tan 90° undefined?
tan = sin ÷ cos, and cos 90° = 0, so the division by zero has no value; the graph has a vertical asymptote there.
What is the ambiguous case?
When you know two sides and an angle that is not between them (SSA), the shorter side can swing to two positions, so there can be two different triangles, one, or none.
Is my data private?
