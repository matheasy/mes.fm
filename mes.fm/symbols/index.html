How to use the symbol copier

  • Click any tile to copy that symbol instantly — a toast confirms it, and it's ready to paste anywhere.
  • Switch the format to LaTeX to copy the command instead of the glyph (e.g. clicking π copies \pi) — handy for MES LaTeX Render or Overleaf. HTML entity copies the numeric character reference (e.g. &#960;) for web pages and email templates.
  • Click several symbols in a row to build a combo in the box at the bottom (like “x²+y²”), then hit Copy all to grab the whole string at once.
  • Use the search box to jump straight to a symbol by name — try “theta”, “infinity”, or “arrow”.
  • Your last several copies show up under Recently copied for quick reuse.

What symbols are included?

Greek letters (lowercase and uppercase) for algebra, geometry and physics — π α β θ φ Δ Σ Ω and the rest of the alphabet; math operators and relations like ± × ÷ ≠ ≤ ≥ ∞ √; superscript and subscript digits and letters for exponents and indices (x² x³ xⁿ a₀); common fractions (½ ⅓ ¼); arrows; set and logic notation (∈ ⊆ ∪ ∩ ∀ ∃); degree and unit signs (° °C °F µ); currency symbols; and general punctuation (© ® ™ § …).

Why not just type it?

Most of these aren't on a standard keyboard, and OS-specific shortcuts (Alt codes on Windows, Option-key combos on Mac) are easy to forget and don't work the same way on mobile. Clicking a tile here works identically on any device, and needs nothing installed.

Is anything sent to a server?

No. The symbol list lives in this page's own JavaScript and the copy happens via your browser's clipboard API — nothing about what you copy or search for leaves your device.
