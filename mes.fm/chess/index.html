    Play chess free: against the computer, a friend, online or on Hive

    A complete chess board in your browser with nothing to install and no account. Play the computer at four levels, a friend on the same phone or computer, or someone online: as a guest with a link, or on the Hive blockchain where every move is signed and kept for good (a live game or slow correspondence chess). Switch between a clean 2D board and a 3D board in a chess club, a tournament hall, a park, a living room or space, with wood, marble, glass or tournament pieces. Variants: Chess960 (Fischer Random), King of the Hill and Three-check.

    How strong is the computer?

    Easy
    Looks one move ahead and adds a lot of chance, so it leaves pieces hanging and misses tactics. Good for learning the moves.
    Medium
    Looks three moves ahead with some chance mixed in: it punishes plain blunders but plays loose positional chess, roughly like a casual club player.
    Hard
    An alpha-beta search with a transposition table, null-move pruning and a quiescence search for about a second (often 8 to 10 half-moves deep), judging positions by material, piece placement, the bishop pair and pawn structure. A real challenge for most club players.
    Expert
    The same engine with three times the thinking time.

    Tick Show the computer's opinion to see an evaluation bar and the best move as an arrow, or press Hint for a single suggestion. The computer plays every variant too.

    How Hive games work

    A Hive game is a series of small signed messages from your Hive account (custom_json operations with the id mes_chess, signed with your posting key, never your active or owner key). The host opens a game with a time per move, the other player joins, and each move is one operation with its move number. Both browsers read both accounts' histories from a Hive node and replay the moves with the same rules, so they always agree and nobody, not even mes.fm, can change a move or the result. Draw offers, resignations and win claims (when the other player runs out of their time per move, judged by block times) are operations too. You can also save the result of a game against the computer or a friend to your account; that is marked as self-reported, because the moves were not signed one by one as they happened.

    Questions

    How do I move?
    Click or tap a piece, then the square it should go to (the dots show where it can go), or drag it there. You can also type moves such as e4, Nf3, exd5, O-O or e8=Q in the box under the board.
    How do I castle?
    Move your king two squares towards the rook (or click the rook after picking the king); the rook jumps over. In Chess960 click the king, then your rook.
    What happens to a pawn on the last rank?
    It is promoted: a small window asks for a queen, rook, bishop or knight.
    When is a game a draw?
    Stalemate (no legal move and not in check), the same position three times, 50 moves by each side without a pawn move or a capture, too little material to mate (such as king and bishop against king), or an agreed draw online.
    What is Chess960?
    Bobby Fischer's variant: the back-rank pieces start in one of 960 shuffled orders (bishops on opposite colours, king between the rooks) and both sides mirror each other. Castling still ends with the king on g1 or c1 and the rook beside it.
    Can I save or share a game?
    Copy a link that opens the game at any move, copy or download it as PGN (the standard format every chess program reads), load a PGN or FEN, save the result to Hive, or write a Hive post about it.
    Can I play on a phone?
    Yes. Fill window and Full screen make the board as big as your screen; held sideways, the board takes the whole height and the moves sit beside it.
