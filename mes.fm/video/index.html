Free Online Video Editor: Cut, Join, Add Titles, Clean Up Sound

Free online video editor in your browser: put videos, sound and pictures on a multi-track timeline, trim and split clips, join many videos in order, add titles and overlays, cross-fade, remove background noise, even out the loudness and export MP4 up to 4K. Opens AVI, MKV, WMV and MOV too. Nothing is uploaded.

A free video editor in your browser

What it does
Put videos, sound and pictures on a timeline with as many video and audio tracks as you need, trim the ends, split at the playhead, delete parts and close the gap, join many clips in order, add titles and picture overlays, cross-fade between clips, fade in and out, change the speed, set the volume of each clip, remove background noise and even out the loudness. Then export an MP4 (H.264 and AAC) that plays everywhere, from 480p to 4K.
Made to replace the everyday parts of Camtasia
Screen recordings and lessons usually need the same few steps: cut out the mistakes, join the parts, put a title on top, make the voice clear and loud enough, export. Those are all here, with the familiar keys (S to split, I and O to mark, J K L to play). AVI, MKV, WMV and other files are converted to MP4 when you import them; an AVI screen recording with H.264 is only re-packed, so it takes seconds.
Clean up the sound
Select a clip and press Remove noise to take out fan hum, hiss and room noise (AI noise removal running in your browser), and Normalize loudness to bring each clip to the same loudness (-16 LUFS for videos and podcasts, -14 for YouTube) without clipping.
Laid out like Camtasia
The library on the left has Media, Titles, Transitions, Audio effects and Visual effects: click a card to use it on the selected clips, or drag it onto a clip. The properties of the selected clip are on the right. Hide either panel or drag its edge to make it wider, and drag the bar above the timeline to give the preview or the timeline more room. The editor remembers the sizes.
Magnetic tracks and separated sound
Press the magnet on a track and its clips close up to the left with no gaps: delete or move a clip and the rest slide along. Separate video and audio puts a clip's sound on its own audio track, linked to the video, so you can see a tall waveform (peaks and average level, on a dB scale so quiet parts show too) and work on the sound alone. Drag the bottom edge of any track to make it taller.
Audio effects that even out the sound
The Leveler brings quiet and loud parts to the same loudness over a few seconds, the Compressor tames the loudest moments, and Ducking lowers music while a voice speaks. They never change your files: they are worked out from the sound as you play and export, the waveform shows the result over the original, and you can change or remove them any time.
Your work is saved as you go
The project and its media are kept in this browser, so closing the tab loses nothing. Save project file writes a small .mesvideo file you can open again later; after the first save (or after opening one) Ctrl+S saves to that same file, and you are warned if it was changed on another computer. The media files are matched by name, so keep them.
Working on more than one computer
Keep your videos in a folder that OneDrive, Dropbox, Google Drive or iCloud syncs and press Link my media folder in the Media panel: the editor shows which files are in it, warns about any that are only in this browser (and can copy them in), and relinks missing ones from it when a project opens. Keep the project file there too, or use the ☁ Cloud button to save it straight to your OneDrive, Dropbox or Google Drive account. The videos are never uploaded by mes.fm.
How the export works
Every frame is decoded with your computer's video decoder (WebCodecs), drawn with the titles and overlays, and encoded again with its H.264 encoder, usually on the graphics chip; the sound is mixed and encoded to AAC. In Chrome, Edge and Brave (with the File System Access API on) the MP4 can be written straight to disk, so even long exports do not fill the memory.
Animations, like Camtasia
Open Animations in the library: zoom in or out, zoom-n-pan to a box you draw on the preview, slide in and out, fade, scale, pop in, spin or a custom move. Each one is an arrow on the clip in the timeline: drag it to move it, drag its ends to change how long it takes, and pick the easing in the properties. Put the playhead at the end of an animation and drag the clip, its corner or its turn handle in the preview to change where it ends up. Animations work on videos, pictures and titles, go into the export exactly as you see them, and are saved in the project.
See the whole frame
The preview shows the frame with a grey margin around it: anything that hangs over the edge is drawn there, darkened, so you can see what is cut off. Zoom out to see more, zoom in to check the edges (Ctrl + wheel, pinch, or the zoom menu), drag the grey area to move around, and press Fit to see it all again. Guides show the title-safe and action-safe areas.
Limits of this version
No green screen or cursor effects yet, and the speed change also changes the pitch of the sound. The compressor works on 10 ms steps, so it evens out speech rather than catching every single sharp peak (the export's limiter stops clipping). Firefox and Safari cannot export (no H.264 encoder there yet); use Chrome, Edge or Brave on a computer.

Is it private?

Yes. Your videos, sound and pictures stay on your computer; nothing is uploaded to mes.fm or anywhere else. The only downloads are the open-source libraries the editor uses (mp4box.js, mp4-muxer, and ffmpeg.wasm when a file needs converting) and the noise-removal model the first time you use it.
