Pick a car and a jump, press Go. The car is a real physics model: springs and dampers on each axle, tyre grip, engine power, air drag, and wheels whose spin turns the body in the air. It takes off, rotates, lands and either rolls away or wrecks, with the g-force the driver would feel.

The equations

Where it is after time t (no air)\( \displaystyle x = v\cos\theta\,t \)\( \displaystyle y = v\sin\theta\,t - \tfrac12 g t^2 \)
Speed needed to cover a gap x landing h below the lip\( \displaystyle v = \sqrt{\frac{g\,x^2}{2\cos^2\theta\,\left(x\tan\theta + h\right)}} \)
Airtime\( \displaystyle t = \frac{v\sin\theta + \sqrt{v^2\sin^2\theta + 2gh}}{g} \)
Longest jump for a given speed (not 45° when you land lower)\( \displaystyle \tan\theta_{\text{best}} = \frac{v}{\sqrt{v^2 + 2gh}} \)
Air drag (slows it, the rest is numerical)\( \displaystyle F_{\text{drag}} = \tfrac12 \rho\, C_d A\, v^2 \)
