8-ball pool, free and with real physics

8-ball is the most played pool game in the world. Break the rack, claim the solids (1 to 7) or the stripes (9 to 15) with your first pot, clear your group and then pot the black 8 to win. This version uses a full 9-foot table and real ball physics, so stun, follow, draw and side spin all work the way they do in a pool hall. Play the computer at five levels (up to Expert), a friend on the same device, or someone online as a guest or on Hive. The MES Billiards page has 9-ball, 10-ball, snooker, practice racks and trick-shot challenges as well.

The rules used here

The break
Break from behind the head string (the dashed line). Any ball potted on the break lets you play on, but the table stays open. The 8 potted on the break is put back on its spot.
Choosing groups
On an open table you may hit any ball except the 8 first. The first ball you pot after the break makes that group yours.
Legal shots
Hit one of your own balls first, then either pot a ball or make any ball touch a cushion.
Fouls
Potting the cue ball, missing every ball, hitting the wrong ball first, or no cushion after the hit. The other player gets ball in hand anywhere on the table.
Winning and losing
Pot the 8 after your group is cleared, without a foul, to win. Potting the 8 early, or in the same shot as a foul, loses the game.

Shots are not called: any legal pot counts. That is how most friendly games are played.

8-ball tips

Hit the break hard and full
Place the cue ball near the middle of the head string and hit the front ball dead centre with nearly full power and a touch of draw so the cue ball stays in the middle of the table.
Plan three balls ahead
Pick the next ball before you shoot and use follow or draw to stop the cue ball where that shot is easy. Leave the ball closest to the 8 for last.
Play safe when nothing is on
A soft shot that hides the cue ball behind your own balls often wins more games than a hopeful long pot.
