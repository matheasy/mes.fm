Where the numbers come from

These are typical room-temperature values for learning and first estimates. They change with the exact alloy, heat treatment, temper, bar size, moisture (wood, nylon), direction (wood, composites, bone) and supplier. Yield and tensile strengths of the A36 steel are the specification minimums. Elongation is in a 50 mm (2 in) gauge length. Reduction of area is a rough figure that only shapes the neck in the tensile test.

Sources: published supplier data sheets (Special Metals for nickel alloys, mill and distributor sheets for steels, aluminium and copper alloys), the ASTM / UNS property collections summarised by MatWeb and MakeItFrom, ASM handbooks, the USDA Forest Products Laboratory Wood Handbook (clear wood at 12% moisture), ACI and Eurocode figures for concrete, textbook tables (Hibbeler, Gere & Goodno, Callister) and biomechanics reviews for bone. For design, use the certified values for your material and the rules of your design code.

For learning and first estimates. Real parts need certified material values, the design code that applies (AISC, Eurocode, ASME…) and a check by a qualified engineer.
