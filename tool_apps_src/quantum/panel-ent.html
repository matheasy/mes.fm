No faster-than-light messages

Can Alice send Bob a message by choosing her angle? Look at Bob’s results split by what Alice chose:

PairsBob got +1Alice got +1

Bob’s +1 rate stays at 50% whatever Alice does (the small wobble is chance and shrinks as pairs pile up). That is true for every angle, exactly: on his own, Bob only ever sees a fair coin. The pattern appears only when the two lists are brought together and compared, and that comparison has to travel by ordinary means, no faster than light. So entanglement gives correlation without signalling.

Why 2 is the limit for hidden variables. Suppose each pair leaves the source carrying instructions: “if your dial is at a, say +1; at a′, say −1”, and so on, and that Alice’s answer cannot depend on Bob’s dial (locality). Then for each single pair the four products A(a)B(b) − A(a)B(b′) + A(a′)B(b) + A(a′)B(b′) = A(a)[B(b) − B(b′)] + A(a′)[B(b) + B(b′)]. One bracket is 0 and the other is ±2, so every pair contributes exactly +2 or −2, and the average can never be bigger than 2. Bell’s 1964 model (each pair carries a random hidden angle; each side answers + if its dial is within 45° of it for photons, 90° for spins) reaches exactly 2 at the best angles, which is as well as any local model can do there. Quantum mechanics reaches 2√2.

What this simulation is. The quantum source draws each pair’s two results together from the joint probabilities above, with a seeded random number generator. That is the honest way to reproduce quantum predictions on one computer; it is not a model of how nature does it. The two hidden-variable sources really are local: Alice’s answer is computed from her own dial and the shared hidden angle only. Detectors here are perfect and every pair is counted, which real experiments had to work hard to approach (see the loopholes below).

The real experiments

YearWhoWhat they did
1935Einstein, Podolsky, RosenArgued that quantum mechanics must be incomplete: perfectly correlated far-apart particles should carry their answers with them.
1964John BellShowed that any such local hidden-variable theory must obey an inequality that quantum mechanics breaks, so the question could be tested.
1969Clauser, Horne, Shimony, HoltThe CHSH form of the inequality (the S ≤ 2 used here), suited to real detectors.
1972Freedman & Clauser (Berkeley)Photon pairs from a calcium atomic cascade: the inequality was violated by about six standard deviations, in agreement with quantum mechanics.
1982Aspect, Grangier, Roger; Aspect, Dalibard, Roger (Orsay)Two-channel polarizers gave S = 2.697 ± 0.015 against the quantum prediction of 2.70. A second experiment switched the settings while the photons were in flight.
1998Weihs, Zeilinger and colleagues (Innsbruck)Settings chosen by fast random generators at each end, far enough apart that no light-speed signal could connect them in time.
2015Hensen et al. (Delft)Electron spins in diamond 1.3 km apart, “loophole-free”: S = 2.42 ± 0.20 from 245 heralded trials.
2015Giustina et al. (Vienna) and Shalm et al. (NIST, Boulder)Photon pairs with high-efficiency detectors and fast random settings, also closing the locality and detection loopholes together (they used the Clauser-Horne / Eberhard form of the inequality).
2022Nobel Prize in PhysicsAlain Aspect, John Clauser and Anton Zeilinger, “for experiments with entangled photons, establishing the violation of Bell inequalities and pioneering quantum information science”.

Loopholes, one line each

  • Locality loophole: if the settings are chosen too slowly, a light-speed signal could tell one side what the other picked. Fix: choose settings at random after the particles leave, far enough apart.
  • Detection (fair-sampling) loophole: if many particles go undetected, the counted ones might be a biased sample. Fix: detect a large enough fraction of all pairs.
  • Freedom-of-choice loophole: the “random” settings might be correlated with the hidden variables. Narrowed by using quantum random generators, distant starlight and even human choices.
  • Memory loophole: hidden variables could use earlier trials; handled by statistics that don’t assume independent trials.

What does it mean? (interpretations)

The experiments settle that no theory with local hidden variables and freely chosen settings matches nature. They do not say which assumption to give up, and physicists still disagree:

Non-locality (e.g. de Broglie-Bohm pilot-wave theory, collapse theories): the measurement on one side really does affect the other, but in a way that cannot be used to send messages. Copenhagen-style views: there were no definite values before measurement; the quantum state is a tool for predicting results. Many worlds: every outcome happens, and the correlation shows up when the branches of Alice and Bob meet. Superdeterminism: the settings were never independent of the particles, so the inequality’s assumption fails. Each fits all the data here; the simulator only shows the predictions.
