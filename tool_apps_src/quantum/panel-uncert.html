Units in this picture: lengths in a unit u, momentum in ℏ/u, time in m·u²/ℏ (so ℏ = m = 1). The calculators below use real units.

Why it happens. To make a short, sharp bump you have to add up waves of many different wavelengths, and wavelength is momentum (p = h/λ). A long, smooth wave needs only a few. So a packet that is well located (small Δx) has a wide spread of momenta (large Δp). This is a property of waves, not of clumsy measuring.

Let it go. A free packet keeps its momentum spread, so its fast parts run ahead of its slow parts and it spreads out. For any packet whose shape starts out real, Δx(t)² = Δx₀² + (Δp t/m)²; for the Gaussian, Δp = ℏ/(2Δx₀), which gives the formula below. The narrower you make it, the faster it spreads.

\( \displaystyle \Delta x\,\Delta p \;\ge\; \frac{\hbar}{2}, \qquad \Delta x(t) = \Delta x_0\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{\hbar t}{2m\,\Delta x_0^{2}}\right)^{2}} \)
Heisenberg’s uncertainty relation (Δ = standard deviation), and the free spreading of a Gaussian packet.
