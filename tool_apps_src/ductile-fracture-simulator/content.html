Every piece of metal is full of tiny voids and hard particles, far too small to see. Pull it and the voids grow, stretch and finally link up into a crack. That is how a ductile metal breaks. The model behind it

The simulator uses the Gurson-Tvergaard-Needleman (GTN) model: the metal is treated as a matrix with a void fraction f, and every step works out how much the voids grow and how much weaker the block gets.

Yield with voids (GTN)\( \displaystyle \Big(\frac{\sigma_e}{\sigma_y}\Big)^2 + 2q_1 f^{*}\cosh\!\Big(\frac{3q_2\,\sigma_m}{2\sigma_y}\Big) - 1 - q_1^2 f^{*2} = 0 \)q₁ = 1.5, q₂ = 1. With f* = 0 this is ordinary von Mises yield.
Void growth\( \displaystyle df = (1-f)\,d\varepsilon_{kk} + A\,d\bar\varepsilon_m + k_\omega\,\omega\, f\, d\varepsilon_e \)Growth (the solid metal keeps its volume), nucleation at particles, and smearing in shear (ω = 1 − ξ²).
Triaxiality across a neck (Bridgman)\( \displaystyle T(\rho) = \frac{1}{3} + \ln\!\Big(1 + \frac{a^2-\rho^2}{2aR}\Big) \)a = neck radius, R = radius of the neck's curve. Highest on the axis: that is why the crack starts in the middle.
Rice-Tracey growth of one void\( \displaystyle \frac{dR}{R} \approx 0.283\, e^{3T/2}\, d\varepsilon_e \)Raise T by 1 and a void grows about 4.5 times faster.

Simplified on purpose: a smooth neck shape stands in for a full finite-element model, the material numbers are rough teaching values tuned to the usual reduction of area, and the shear lips of the cup and cone are drawn once two thirds of the section has cracked. Good for seeing the trends, not for designing parts.

The video this is based on

“How and When Metals Fail” (Cornell University, 2013 IGERT video competition). Jeffrey Bomarito explains that every block of metal holds microscopic voids, that they grow and link when it is pulled, shows a copper bar necking, the dimples on a fracture surface, and a computer model of a notched bar cracking from the centre outwards.

This page rebuilds those ideas so you can try them: the bar above (copper, or a notched bar), the dimpled fracture surface, and one void loaded in different ways.
