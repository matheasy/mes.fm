Rule of 72 vs the exact doubling time

Exact = ln 2 ÷ ln(1 + yearly growth), with the compounding chosen above. The rule of 72 is a mental shortcut; it is best between about 6% and 10%.

The formulas

Where they come from, step by step, with worked examples and practice questions: Compound Interest Explained.

one amount \( \displaystyle A = P\left(1+\frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt} \)
continuous \( \displaystyle A = P\,e^{rt} \)
regular deposits \( \displaystyle A = P(1+i)^{N} + D\,\frac{(1+i)^{N}-1}{i} \)
yearly growth (APY) \( \displaystyle \text{APY} = \left(1+\frac{r}{n}\right)^{n}-1 \)
doubling time \( \displaystyle t_2 = \frac{\ln 2}{\ln(1+\text{APY})} \approx \frac{72}{100\,r} \)

P = starting amount, r = yearly rate as a decimal (5% = 0.05), n = compounding periods per year, t = years, D = deposit per period, i = growth per deposit period (with monthly compounding and monthly deposits i = r/12), N = number of deposits. Deposits at the start of each period earn one more period: multiply the deposit part by (1 + i).
