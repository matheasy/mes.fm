APR (annual percentage rate) is the yearly rate that makes the payments worth exactly the money you really got. Fees make it higher than the interest rate, more so on short loans. Some fees (late fees, optional insurance) are not part of the official APR.

The formulas

payment \( \displaystyle M = L\,\frac{i}{1-(1+i)^{-n}} \)
loan from a payment \( \displaystyle L = M\,\frac{1-(1+i)^{-n}}{i} \)
each payment \( \displaystyle \text{interest}_k = i\,B_{k-1},\quad B_k = B_{k-1} - (M - \text{interest}_k) \)
APR \( \displaystyle \text{received} = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{M_k}{(1+j)^k},\quad \text{APR} = j \times \text{payments per year} \)

L = loan amount, i = yearly rate ÷ payments per year (6% monthly: i = 0.005), n = number of payments, B = balance. The payment is rounded to the cent and interest is rounded to the cent every payment, so the last payment is a few cents different and clears the balance to exactly 0.00.
