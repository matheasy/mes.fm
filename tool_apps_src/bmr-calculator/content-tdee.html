      Calories at every activity level

      Your BMR through life

      The same weight and height at every age. Resting burn drifts down with age (about 5 kcal a day per year with Mifflin-St Jeor), mostly because of lost muscle, so keeping muscle matters.

      What is TDEE (total daily energy expenditure)?

      Your TDEE is the total number of calories you burn in a day: your resting burn (BMR), plus the energy to digest food, plus everything you do, from walking to the gym. Eat about your TDEE and your weight stays steady; eat less and you lose weight; eat more and you gain. It is also called your maintenance calories.

      How this TDEE calculator works

      It calculates your BMR from your age, sex, weight and height, then multiplies by an activity factor: TDEE = BMR × activity multiplier. The default equation is Mifflin-St Jeor; you can switch to Harris-Benedict, or to Katch-McArdle or Cunningham if you know your body-fat %.

      Example: a 30-year-old man at 80 kg and 180 cm has a BMR of 1,780 kcal. Moderately active (×1.55), his TDEE is about 2,759 kcal a day. To lose about 0.5 kg a week he would eat roughly 2,209 kcal (a 550 kcal deficit).

      Which activity level should I pick?

      Most people overestimate. Count only deliberate exercise, and choose the lower level if you are between two. A desk job with three workouts a week is usually lightly active. You can then check the estimate against your weight: if you hold the same weight for 2–3 weeks on your TDEE in calories, it is right; if not, adjust by 100–200 kcal.

      Using your TDEE to lose or gain weight

      About 7,700 kcal is one kilogram of body weight (3,500 per pound), so a 500 kcal daily deficit loses roughly 0.5 kg (1 lb) a week. The calculator shows each target, flags any that fall below your BMR or the usual 1,200 (women) and 1,500 (men) minimums, and the “What if I eat…?” slider projects your weight week by week, including how the rate slows as your weight drops.

      Questions

      What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?
      BMR is what you burn at complete rest; TDEE is your whole day including movement. TDEE is the number to eat by.
      Is TDEE accurate?
      It is an estimate, usually within 10% for healthy adults. Track your weight for a few weeks and adjust.
      Should I eat back my exercise calories?
      Not if your activity level already includes that exercise. Choose the level that matches your usual week and keep the calories steady.
