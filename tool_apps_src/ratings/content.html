    How the ranking works

    The fair average. A page with one 5-star rating should not beat a page with 200 ratings averaging 4.8. So every page starts as if it already had 5 ratings of 4 stars, and its real ratings are added to those: score = (sum of stars + 4 × 5) ÷ (number of ratings + 5). With few ratings a page sits near 4; with many, the score is its real average. This is called a Bayesian average.

    One vote per person. Each browser has one vote per page; rating again replaces your earlier rating. Only a few different browsers from one internet connection can rate the same page in 30 days (a school or office shares one connection), and there is an hourly limit, so a script cannot stuff the results.

    Privacy. A rating stores the page, the stars, a random number your browser makes up for itself, and for 30 days a scrambled (hashed) form of your internet address, never the address itself. No text you write is stored. See the privacy policy.
