See what a website stores in your browser

Websites can keep data in your browser: small settings in localStorage, projects and files in IndexedDB, downloaded files in the Cache storage, private files in the origin private file system, and cookies. MES.fm's tools use these to remember your settings, autosave your work (Photo Editor, Video Editor, Audio Editor, 3D Studio, Copy Text …) and keep big AI models so they download only once. This page measures all of it for mes.fm, names the tool each piece belongs to, and lets you delete what you don't need.

Questions

Can this page see other websites' data?
No. Browsers keep every site's storage apart, so a page sees only its own. To see every site, use the browser settings page listed in the guide above.
What is the "limit" number?
It is how much the browser would let mes.fm store (its quota), usually worked out from your disk size. It is not your free disk space. Chrome, Edge and Brave allow a site up to about 60% of the disk; Firefox up to 10% of the disk or 10 GB (more once data is kept as "persistent"); Safari 17 and later up to about 60%, older Safari about 1 GB.
What does "Keep this data" do?
When the disk gets full, browsers may delete website data they think is unused. Asking to keep it (persistent storage) tells the browser not to delete mes.fm's data on its own; it still goes when you clear site data. Chrome grants it to sites you use often or have bookmarked or installed; Firefox asks you.
Why does the memory section show only this tab?
Chrome, Edge, Brave and Opera tell a page roughly how much JavaScript memory it uses (rounded, to protect privacy). Total memory, other tabs and extensions are only in the browser's task manager (Shift+Esc) or your system's. Firefox and Safari don't report a page's memory at all.
Is it safe to delete?
Deleting a tool's data resets that tool: settings go back to the defaults and autosaved work is lost. Downloaded models download again the next time you use the feature. Nothing on your disk outside the browser is touched.
What about the browser's normal cache?
The ordinary web cache (pages, pictures and scripts kept to load faster) is managed by the browser and hidden from pages, so it isn't listed here. Clear it in the browser's "Clear browsing data" window.
