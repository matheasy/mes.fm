Why OBS shows your mic at max but you still sound quiet

There are three volume steps between your mouth and your stream, and only the first two can make you louder:

  1. The microphone's own gain. A Blue Yeti has a gain knob on the back. This is the best place to set your level.
  2. The computer's input volume. On a Mac: System Settings > Sound > Input, pick the mic, and move the Input volume slider. This is applied before OBS sees the sound. If it is low, everything after it is low.
  3. The OBS mixer fader. Its top is 0.0 dB, which means "no change". It can only turn you down, never up. So "the fader is at max" does not mean "loud".

OBS's meter shows the level in dBFS after its fader. This page shows the level the browser gets from the mic, which is the same point OBS starts from.

How to get louder, in this order

  1. Turn the Yeti's gain knob up until your normal voice sits in the target band (or the Yeti's own light stays green).
  2. Raise the macOS Input volume (System Settings > Sound > Input). Many Macs set a USB mic quite low the first time.
  3. Still quiet? In OBS, right-click the mic > Filters > + > Gain and add a few dB. Add a Limiter at about -3 dB after it so loud laughs don't clip.

Speak 10-20 cm (4-8 inches) from the mic, into the side of a Yeti (it is a side-address mic), not the top.

Questions

What is dBFS?

Decibels relative to "full scale", the loudest sample a recording can store. 0 dBFS is the ceiling; everything else is negative. -6 dBFS is about half the maximum, -20 dBFS is a tenth.

Why turn off automatic gain control?

Automatic gain control (AGC) quietly turns your voice up and down for you. Video-call apps like Zoom, Meet and Discord use it, so a quiet mic can sound fine there and still be quiet in OBS, which has no AGC. Test with it off to see your real level. In Zoom, untick "Automatically adjust microphone volume" if it fights your settings.

Peak or average: which number matters?

Both. The average (RMS) is how loud you sound; the peak is how close you come to clipping. A good voice level has an average around -18 to -12 dBFS and peaks around -6.

Why does the browser ask for the microphone?

It has to, to hear anything. The sound stays in this tab: nothing is recorded unless you press Record, and the recording stays on your computer.

The list has no names, only "Microphone 1"?

Browsers show device names only after you allow the microphone once. Press Start, allow it, and the names appear.
