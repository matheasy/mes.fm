Smash your brick builds

Build anything from virtual bricks, a house, a castle, a tower, then press 💥 Smash at the top and destroy it. Ram it with a truck, swing a wrecking ball into it, drop it from high up, or build a car and crash it into a wall. Watch it in slow motion and see which studs let go and how many pieces fly off. It all runs in your browser, with nothing to install.

How to smash something

  1. Build something on the baseplate (or open an example), then press 💥 Smash at the top left. 🧱 Build takes you back, with your build untouched.
  2. Pick how to smash it: Ram it (a truck drives into it), Wrecking ball, Drop it, or Crash a car into a concrete wall, a brick wall, a foam wall or another car head-on.
  3. Choose what gets smashed: My build, or a ready-made house, castle, robot, wall and more. Set the speed, the ball size, the swing or the height.
  4. Press 💥 Smash it! (or the space bar). Press ↺ Put it back, change one thing and smash it again.

Why do some builds smash and others survive?

At the moment of impact every part has to be stopped almost at once, and the force to stop it has to come from the wall through the bricks in front of it or through the studs that hold it. Pushing on the brick in front never breaks anything, but each stud joint only grips with about a newton or two. A tall stack of bricks held by one or two studs lets go easily; parts locked in by overlapping plates and bricks are held by many studs and stay on. That is the same idea as a real car's strong passenger cell.

Questions

Can I crash my own build?
Yes: build it in the Build tab, then choose "My build" as the vehicle. If it drives sideways, press "Turn the vehicle".
What does "like a drop from" mean?
Crashing at that speed hits as hard as dropping the car from that height (h = v² / 2g). 15 km/h is like a drop of almost 90 cm.
Is it real physics?
It is a simple model for fun and learning: the parts are stopped in 1.5 to 20 thousandths of a second depending on the wall, stud joints let go above their grip, and the loose pieces fly, bounce and spin. Real bricks also bend and crack, which is not shown.
