Contraction timer for labour

A simple contraction timer made for a tired hand: one big button. Tap it when a contraction starts and again when it ends. The timer records how long each one lasted and how far apart they are, shows your average over the last hour, and tells you when your contractions have followed the 5-1-1 rule (every 5 minutes, lasting 1 minute, for 1 hour) so you know when it is time to call.

How to use it

  • Start at the first sign of a contraction, Stop when it eases. The gap is measured from the start of one contraction to the start of the next.
  • Your log is saved on this phone even if you close the page. Tap Keep the screen on so it does not go to sleep.
  • Forgot to tap? Add one by hand. Tapped by mistake? Undo or remove a row.
  • Use Copy log or Download CSV to show your midwife or doctor.

Questions

What is the 5-1-1 rule?

Contractions about 5 minutes apart, each lasting about 1 minute, for at least 1 hour. It is a typical signal to head to the hospital or birth centre for a first baby. Some providers use 4-1-1 or 3-1-1, especially if you live far away or have had a quick labour before. Choose the one your provider gave you.

How do I tell Braxton Hicks from real contractions?

Braxton Hicks contractions are irregular, usually ease with rest, water or changing position, and do not get closer together. Real labour contractions become longer, stronger and closer together and do not go away when you move or rest.

When should I call right away?

If your water breaks, you have bleeding, the baby is moving much less, the pain is severe or constant, or you are less than 37 weeks and having regular contractions.
