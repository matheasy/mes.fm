How it is worked out

  • Positions. Where the Sun, Moon and planets were, seen from Earth, comes from Astronomy Engine (accurate to well under a minute of arc), on the ecliptic of the date. The signs are the tropical zodiac used by Western astrology: 30° each, starting at the March equinox.
  • Time zone. Your local birth time is turned into world time with your browser’s time-zone database, daylight saving included; the offset used is shown under the form. For an old date or an unusual place, type the offset yourself (for example +05:30).
  • Moon sign without a time. The Moon changes sign every two to three days. If it did so on your birthday, the page says so and gives both signs with the time of the change.
  • Rising sign and houses. The ascendant is the point of the zodiac rising on the eastern horizon at your birth time and place; it moves through all twelve signs in a day, so it needs both. Houses are whole-sign (house 1 = the whole rising sign) or equal (30° from the ascendant): simple systems that work at any latitude. Placidus and Koch are not offered.
  • Chinese zodiac. The Chinese year starts at Lunar New Year (a new moon between 21 January and 20 February), not on 1 January, so people born in January or early February often belong to the previous year’s animal.

Questions

Why is my sign different on another website?

Near a sign change (a “cusp”) the exact day moves by a day between years, and many sites use fixed dates. This page works it out for your actual date and time. Sidereal (Vedic) astrology uses a zodiac shifted by about 24°, so its signs are usually one sign earlier.

Are the zodiac signs the same as the constellations?

No. The signs were named after constellations about 2,000 years ago; since then the slow wobble of Earth’s axis has shifted the sky by almost a whole sign, so on your birthday the Sun was usually in the constellation before your sign.

Is anything I type stored?

No. Everything is computed in your browser, and the share link only exists if you copy it.
